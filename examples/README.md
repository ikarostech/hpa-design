# HPADesign sample project

`HPADesign-Aero-Structural-MVP.json` is an importable schema-version 5 project demonstrating the current MVP:

- wing geometry, airfoils, and polar data;
- a completed VLM sweep with spanwise lift, drag, and pitching-torque distributions;
- a carbon-tube spar and an aerodynamic load case linked to the default maximum-lift VLM operating point at `alpha = 14 deg`;
- a completed beam analysis with bending/torsion capacity and reserve-factor distributions;
- matching aerodynamic and structural results ready for the integrated charts on the Results page.

The application keeps this file as an importable example. A valid browser recovery document remains authoritative and is not overwritten.

Regenerate the file after intentional solver or mock-input changes with:

```powershell
npx jiti scripts/generateSampleProject.ts
```

`Anonymized-HPA-Reference.json` is an importable schema-version 6 project converted from anonymized human-powered-aircraft design records. Aircraft, airfoil, analysis-case, and material identifiers have been replaced with generic labels for public distribution. The project contains a main-wing airfoil family, Re=450,000 XFoil polars, spanwise planform, incidence, a completed viscous-corrected LLT sweep from -3 to 5 degrees at 7.4 m/s, a legacy XFLR5 trim point, the ordered spar inner diameters, the spanwise partial-ply schedule, and a net wing load distribution at 1.5 G. Completed aerodynamic, structural, and coupled results are embedded. The coupled result uses 7.4 m/s and 5 degrees and converged after 37 iterations. Its wing-tip deflection exceeds 10% of the half-span, so the saved result warns that the linear beam model may be outside its range of applicability. This is the fresh default project. Four XFoil polars are marked for review because one or two points outside or at the edge of the LLT operating range did not converge; their converged points remain available to LLT. Material properties absent from the source records are identified as provisional in the material notes.

The default project's elevator and rudder rib stations come from `翼設計.xlsx`, sheets `エレベーターリブ構成` (`A2:B7`) and `ラダーリブ構成` (`A2:B11`). Positions and chords were converted from mm to m. The rudder is one vertical surface from its -600 mm station to its +2100 mm station, so its model root is the lower end. The workbook's 4.3 m elevator and 5.15 m rudder moment arms (`物性・制約条件!B46` and `B38`) determine root X positions relative to the sample wing's quarter-MAC. For the rudder, the wing quarter-MAC includes the wing's leading-edge offsets; its root leading edge is set from the wing quarter-MAC so the rudder quarter-chord spar lies 5.15 m aft of that reference. The corrected chord at Z=1800 mm is 660 mm. Each rudder section uses X Offset = (680 mm - chord) / 4, placing its quarter-chord on the same straight spar line. The elevator root leading edge is set so its quarter-chord spar lies 4.3 m aft of the wing quarter-MAC; each section uses X Offset = (840 mm - chord) / 4. The exact workbook datum for the stated moment arms remains to be verified. The workbook does not specify the tail airfoil; following the user's NACA0042 designation, the sample generates a closed-trailing-edge NACA0042 profile with the application's NACA 4-digit generator and assigns it to both surfaces. The workbook does not define incidence, twist, or vertical installation height; the sample uses zero angles and the documented root height. Both tail leading-edge offset sets follow the user-specified straight quarter-chord spar. Verify those assumptions before interpreting the standalone loads. Four complete NACA0042 XFOIL polars cover Re 200k, 300k, 450k, and 600k at Mach 0.03, Ncrit 9, and alpha -4° to 8° in 1° steps. They were generated with `node scripts/generateReferenceTailPolars.mjs` and cover the tail sections at 7.4 and 10 m/s for the default 2° angle. A completed whole-aircraft VLM case at 7.4 m/s and 2 degrees includes both tail surfaces and saves their surface loads. The Tail Design page displays geometry only. This VLM result is inviscid (its CD is induced drag only); the NACA0042 XFOIL polars remain available for future analysis. Earlier wing-only and coupled results were not recalculated for the new tail geometry.


The reference sample records a main-wing spar position of 35% local chord as a provisional estimate of the section center of pressure near 5 degrees angle of attack. At Re=450,000, linear interpolation of its saved 4- and 6-degree XFOIL polars gives approximately 33–36% chord across the main-wing airfoil family, using x_cp/c = 0.25 - Cm,quarter-chord/Cl. The elevator and rudder retain their documented straight quarter-chord spars. The source workbook does not identify the main wing's chordwise spar location, and the center of pressure moves with operating condition. Changing the setting alone does not move the planform or change the saved aerodynamic solution; using the alignment action changes section X offsets and requires a new analysis. The FSI elastic-axis input remains independent of this spar-position setting.
